Measured against the original game, and brought back in line with it.
  A new check (tests/vanilla.mjs) loads the game twice, once with this mod blocked, which is

  Skills cost exactly what the original charges, for the first ten levels. The mod's old curve

  range he designed: all 22 of his perk ladders end between level 1 and 15. From level 10 it
  rises about 3% a level, at a rate worked out so level 110 is exactly as far away as it was
  Why not match him to 15, his last perk? His level 15 alone costs 823,099 xp, and ninety-five
  more levels even at that flat price is two and a half times the whole budget.
  Skill exp is 1x and enemy coin drops are off, the original's values. Both were raised by this
  mod (2x, 15%). The coin drop is the author's own mechanism, switched on only by the Coin Ring
  and the Ring of Greed; at 15% under them those two rings were worthless. Both settings
  boxes still work, and anything you set there yourself still wins

  curve before they existed, so level 20 cost 7,474,050 xp instead of 375 and their perks to
  110 could not be reached
  Fixed: about one spawn in twenty came out much weaker than intended. Enemy strength is set by
  sampling 32 of your swings, and when none of them crit the crits were not averaged badly, they
  were left out entirely, and they are 30% of your damage. Those spawns were ~20% short. Now
  98% of spawns land within 4% of their intended fight length
  The same bug in the balance tests is also why they sometimes failed on a rank duel and
  passed on a re-run: 0.15% of the time they read a fight 40% longer than it was.
  Not matched, on purpose: fight length. At equal progression the original's early fights take
  one or two swings and almost never hurt you, because you out-level its fixed creatures. This
  mod holds every fight near 9 swings and 22 to die. The test prints that gap

----MOD 4.1:
[2026/09/26]
Skill rarity, and convergence — fold a whole section into one skill.
  Every skill has a rarity now, ten of them from Novice to Transcendent, and the
  skill list paints each row in its colour
  It is the rank of the level you have taken the skill to — the same thresholds and
  the same ten colours titles use. Derived, not assigned: a hand-picked rarity per skill
  would be a hundred numbers to keep in step with a ladder that moves, and would say
  nothing about your character.
  Jade Slips (玉简) fold a skill into its section. Fold every skill in a section and it
  is one line on your sheet instead of up to twenty-five — Warfare, Constitution,
  Pathfinding, Livelihood, Workmanship, Fortitude, Attunement, Provision, Maintenance,
  Fellowship
  The Slip Archive is on the dojo lobby, beside Level Advancement. It sells the slips;
  the price rises with every one you have spent, so converging all hundred is a project
  Folding costs you nothing but the slip. A folded skill keeps its level, keeps earning
  exp, keeps every perk and keeps applying every buff it ever applied. It is hidden, not
  removed, which is also why it can be unpicked at any time
  Hiding rather than removing is the whole design. Shortening the skill list breaks three
  things at once: the game's panel only redraws when the list GROWS, so removed rows would
  stay on screen forever; the game pushes a skill back onto the list on its next level-up,
  banner and all, so a fold would undo itself; and the list is saved by id. The mod's
  buffs were always applied from the skills themselves, never from the list — so "all
  of the buffs" is literally true rather than re-implemented. Measured: every stat after
  folding all hundred is exactly its unfolded value times the convergence bonus.
  A converged section is worth x1.03 on every stat, compounding — x1.34 with all ten.
  A partly folded one pays a share in proportion, so it rewards you as you go
  A Skill handbook in the wiki, beside the skills page: the rarity ladder, which skills
  fold into which, where you stand, and what the slips cost
  New: modConverge(), modRarity(), modFold(section), modUnfold(section).
  tests/convergence.mjs, 43 checks. tests/perkcoverage.mjs no longer grades the ten
  converged skills — they are never trained, so a perk ladder on them could never be
  climbed, the same reason Renown was already exempt.

----MOD 4.0:
[2026/09/25]
The cultivator's road — the realm ladder finally has a road between its rungs.
  Sourced, not invented. Wuxiaworld's "General Glossary of Terms in Wuxia, Xianxia &
  Xuanhuan Novels" is the genre's own reference work and it names every piece below.
  Its entry on bottlenecks was the design brief: breaking one can take new insight, a
  medicinal pill, or harsher training.
  The mod shipped with one of those three. All three are here now.
  Spiritual Root (灵根) — rolled once, when the dojo finishes teaching you to
  fight. Five grades by purity, commonest worst. Never a dead end: the worst costs 15%
  cultivation speed, not access, and a Root Cleansing Pill moves you up a grade
  Nine layers to every realm (层) — derived from where Qi Circulation sits between
  this realm's threshold and the next, so breaking through lands you at the first layer
  of the new one on its own. You are now "Core Formation, 5th layer (middle stage)", or
  at the Peak of it, or a half step to Nascent Soul — which is the genre's own name
  for the state this mod has been calling a bottleneck
  Insight (参悟) — the half of the price that cannot be bought, from the three
  sources the glossary names and no fourth of our own: meditating, a kill made under
  20% health, and the first time you set foot in an area. Spent whether the attempt
  works or not, because what you spend is understanding
  Closed Door Training (闭关) — a fifth action, and the only one that costs you
  something: it holds the action timer, and it refuses to start unless you are actually
  at a wall. Fifteen minutes in it is worth +30% on the attempt
  And it shelters you. The glossary gives seclusion two purposes, breaking a
  bottleneck and not being disturbed at the crucial moment and suffering a backlash
  — so a breakthrough attempted while you are still sitting in it cannot
  deviate, only fail. Sit and be safe, or push it in the open and risk it.
  Qi Deviation (走火入魔) — failing in the open now leaves your cultivation base
  unstable for half an in-game day: stats at x0.55, body at x0.70, no second attempt
  until it passes. Clears on its own or at once with a Qi Settling Pill. Never kills
  Heavenly Tribulation (天劫) — realm 10 was called Tribulation Transcendence and
  there was no tribulation. From realm 8 up, a successful breakthrough is answered by
  lightning: 3, 5 then 7 bolts costing 55/70/85% of your max HP. A gate on walking in
  healthy, anchored to the player like every other number here. Fall and the realm
  does not open
  Impurities (杂质) — something black and foul-smelling leaves you on the way up
  New: modRoad() and modRoots() in the console; two pills at the Herbalist; the wiki's
  Cultivation page carries all of it, generated from the live tables as ever.

Fixed: a realm's body multiplier did nothing at all.
  Found while building the above. Section 28 raised max HP and max energy by ASSIGNING
  you.hpmax and you.satmax — the exact mistake v3.9 fixed for hpTrack, still sitting
  in the realm code. stat_r recomputes BOTH from their inputs, so both were undone on
  the next call. Measured on a realm-10 character: hpmax 332 after allbuff and 39 after
  one stat_r; satmax 1700, then 200. The x8.5 the top realm advertised lasted one call.
  Ten realms of health and energy you were never given, you now have. Both go
  through hpm and satm, which are inputs to stat_r and therefore survive
  Two contributors wanted the same two fields, and two delta-trackers on one field
  cannot work — each recovers its base by dividing out its own factor and finds the
  other's sitting in it. So nothing writes hpm/satm any more: contributors publish a
  factor to MOD_BODY and one idempotent applier writes the product, one delta flag per
  axis. Verified: survives stat_r, zero drift over 200 calls, Mortal untouched.

----MOD 3.9:
[2026/09/23]
Fixed: you never actually had the max HP the game was giving you.
  The mod raises max HP with your offence (hpTrack). It did that by ASSIGNING you.hpmax —
  and stat_r recomputes hpmax from (hp_r + hpa) * hpm * hpe on the very next call, which
  the tick, combat, and allbuff itself all make constantly. Measured on a cap-60
  character: one stat_r() took max HP from 1,492,947 to 72,377. Sampled every 137ms
  across six seconds of play, the low number appeared in all 43 samples.
  Enemy damage was sized against the number you never held. MOD_scaleEnemy reads
  you.hpmax in the instant after allbuff, while the assignment still stands, so every
  fight was built for a character with ~18x the health you actually had. Harmless early,
  worst at the cap
  It writes the multiplier now, not the total — hpm is an input to stat_r, so it
  survives. Max HP holds through 200 consecutive stat_r calls
  hpm is SAVED, so the mod's factor is tracked in global.flags and divided back out
  rather than multiplied in again — the same pairing the coin-drop rate uses, and the
  same trap you.res once fell into. Verified across six save/load cycles and 20,000
  allbuff calls: zero drift.
  Your fights are now as deadly as they were always meant to be, which is to say
  roughly eighteen times less so than they have been
Two corrections found by the tests, not by reading:
  the recovered base needed flooring at 1 — anything resetting hpm without clearing the

Also: tests/run.sh now serves with -c-1. Without it http-server sends max-age=3600 and

  once during this work as an allareas failure that would not reproduce standalone.
  A flaky safety net is worse than a failing one.
Tests. tests/targets.mjs gains 5 checks — max HP survives one stat_r and two hundred,

  All 2,790 allareas matchups still pass — they were measuring the right number all
  along. It was play that had the wrong one.

Tooling, not gameplay: the balance numbers now come with error bars.
  Every balance figure in the suite is a sample mean of something heavy-tailed — crit rate
  reaches 33% and a crit is ~8x a normal swing. Until now the scripts reported point
  estimates: fightsmoke medians nine duels, allareas prints one number per matchup, and

  to conclude anything — and says which

  median of 2 swings carries an interval of [0.46, 4.87], ±83%. That number was being
  read as a measurement and it is noise. Flagged in place rather than failed

  whiff 0. None checked whether the model hits its own dials, which a single matchup
  cannot tell you. 45 spawns an area, compared against the target THAT SPAWN was solved
  for, with an interval. Catches the model drifting without breaking the ordering
  Verified it fails: a 15% drift injected into _modDmg is caught at 0.869 of target.
  Result: the model is on its dials — every area tracks its per-spawn kill and die
  targets to within 1%, and kill < die holds on 45/45 spawns in all four areas sampled
Four corrections along the way, all to the new test, none to the mod:

----MOD 3.3:
[2026/09/15]
Fixed: every title was listed twice after loading a save.
  Found by auditing, not reported. giveTitle pushes each earned title onto TWO arrays —
  global.titles and global.titlese — and load() rebuilds global.titles from the save
  BY INDEX and then appends the whole of titlese on top. So everything earned that
  session ends up in the array twice. Measured on a character holding 241 titles: 241
  before a load, 481 after, 241 distinct.
  It does not compound — the next load finds titlese empty — but one load is enough,
  and save() writes the inflated array straight back out
  Both title screens are affected: the game's own, and the mod's grouped picker. Both
  iterate global.titles, so each title got two rows
  A base-game bug, but the mod is what makes it loud: vanilla hands out around a hundred
  titles and section 24 grants five per skill, so the doubling is hundreds of rows.
  Existing saves are repaired on load, by identity, the same way the skill sheet is —
  global.titles holds references to the objects in ttl, so the same object twice is
  always wrong whatever put it there. Not fixed at the source: titlese is the author's

Found, not fixed: the player's real max HP is not the one the balance model uses.
